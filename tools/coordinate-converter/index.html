Coordinate Converter

Paste a location in almost any format and get it back in all the others. Decimal degrees, degrees-minutes-seconds, degrees and decimal minutes, Plus Codes and geohashes, each with a copy button.

Runs in your browser, uploads nothing. What you type stays on this device. The converter itself makes no network requests. A map link sends the location to that map service only when you open it.

    How to use it

    1. Paste or type a location into the box. The converter works it out as you type.
    2. Check the green line under the box. It says which format the converter read.
    3. If it guessed wrong, pick the right format under Read as.
    4. Click Copy next to the format you need, or open the location in a map.

    Formats it reads

    • Decimal degrees. 40.7128, -74.0060, 40.7128 -74.0060 or 40.7128° N, 74.0060° W. Latitude comes first unless N, S, E and W letters say otherwise.
    • Degrees, minutes and seconds. 40°26'46"N 79°58'56"W, 40 26 46 N 79 58 56 W, N 40 26 46 W 79 58 56 or 40d26m46s N, 79d58m56s W. Curly quotes and the prime marks ′ and ″ work too.
    • Degrees and decimal minutes. N 40° 26.767' W 079° 58.933' or 41 24.2028, 2 10.4418.
    • Plus Codes. Full codes such as 8FVC9G8F+6W, in capitals or lower case. Short codes such as 9G8F+6W need a nearby place, explained in the FAQ.
    • Geohashes. Up to 12 characters, such as u4pruydqqvj.
    • Map links. Full Google Maps, Apple Maps and OpenStreetMap links, and geo: links. Short links such as maps.app.goo.gl cannot be read here. Reading one means asking Google where it leads, and this page sends nothing.
    • Negative numbers and letters. A minus sign means south or west. So does the letter S or W, written before or after the number.

    Separate latitude and longitude with a comma, a semicolon or a space. If you use decimal commas, as in 48,8584; 2,2945, put a semicolon between the two numbers.

    For developers

    Location tools disagree about the order of the two numbers. Most people and most map apps write latitude first. GeoJSON (RFC 7946) writes longitude first. So does the Android Emulator console command geo fix, according to Google's emulator console guide. Copy the "Longitude first" line when a tool wants that order. To turn a list of points into a route file for Xcode or the Android Emulator, use the free Simulator GPX Builder.

    FAQ

    What is the difference between decimal degrees and degrees, minutes and seconds?

    They are two ways to write the same angle. Decimal degrees use one number, such as 40.446111. Degrees, minutes and seconds (DMS) split that number up. One degree is 60 minutes, and one minute is 60 seconds, so 40.446111 north is 40°26'46"N. Degrees and decimal minutes (DDM) stop at minutes, as in 40°26.7667'N. All three name the same point.

    Does latitude or longitude come first?

    Latitude usually comes first. Google Maps Help tells you to enter latitude before longitude. Some formats reverse the order. GeoJSON puts longitude first, and so does the Android Emulator's geo fix command. If a latitude comes out above 90, the two numbers are probably the wrong way round. To read them in the other order, choose "Longitude, latitude" under "Read as".

    What do negative numbers mean?

    A negative latitude is south of the equator. A negative longitude is west of the prime meridian, the line of 0° longitude that runs through Greenwich in London. So -33.8688, 151.2093 is the same place as 33.8688 S, 151.2093 E. Use a minus sign or a letter, not both.

    What is a Plus Code?

    A Plus Code is a short code for a place, such as 8FVC9G8F+6W. The open system behind it is called Open Location Code, and Google Maps can search for these codes. A full code has 8 characters, a plus sign, and then 2 or more characters. A 10-digit code names an area about 14 by 14 metres at the equator. An 11th digit narrows that to about 2.8 by 3.5 metres. The codes use 20 characters, picked to reduce writing errors and to avoid spelling words.

    Why does a short Plus Code need a nearby place?

    A short code such as 9G8F+6W leaves out the first characters of the full code. Many places on Earth share the same ending, so the short code alone does not say which one you mean. Google Maps usually shows a town name after a short code to fill that gap. This page does not look up place names, because that would mean sending them to a map service. Instead, enter the coordinates of any point near the code, and the converter finds the nearest match.

    What is a geohash?

    A geohash is a short string of letters and digits that names an area, such as u4pruydqqvj. Gustavo Niemeyer published the system in 2008 and put it in the public domain. Each extra character makes the area smaller. Geohashes use 32 characters: the digits 0 to 9 and the letters of the alphabet except a, i, l and o. Places whose geohashes share a longer start are close together. The reverse is not always true: two close places can have quite different geohashes.

    How precise are the results?

    Decimal degrees are shown to 6 decimal places, which is about 11 centimetres of latitude. Seconds are shown to 2 decimal places, about 31 centimetres. Decimal minutes are shown to 4 places, about 19 centimetres. Longitude steps get shorter away from the equator, so they are finer still. A Plus Code or a geohash names an area, not a point. The converter shows the centre of that area and how big it is.

    Does this tool send my location anywhere?

    No. The converter runs in your browser and does not upload what you type. "Use my location" asks your browser for your position and shows it only on this page. How the browser works out that position depends on the browser and the device. A map link sends the location to Apple, Google or OpenStreetMap only when you open it.

    Need your iPhone or Android to report one of these locations?

    Wander can set it. In the app, paste coordinates or a Plus Code, or tap a spot on the map. Some apps' terms forbid changing your location, so check the terms of the apps you use it with.
