Precise Location vs Approximate Location: What Apps Really Get, and When to Turn Precise Off

Precise location gives an app your exact spot (usually within about 50 metres on Android), while approximate location gives it only a rough area: about 3 square kilometres on Android, and usually within 1 to 20 kilometres of you on iPhone. Turn Precise off for apps that only need your town, like weather, news and shopping, and leave it on for maps, ride-hailing and delivery.

Quick answer

A location permission is the access you give one app to read where your phone is. Both iPhone and Android split that permission in two. Precise location gives the app your exact spot. Approximate location gives it only a rough area around you, so it may know your town but not your street.

  • Turn Precise off for apps that only need your town: weather, news, shopping and most social apps.
  • Leave Precise on for apps that must find your exact spot: maps and navigation, ride-hailing, food delivery, run and ride tracking, and anything that alerts you when you arrive somewhere.

The setting is free, works one app at a time, and switches back in seconds.

What each setting actually shares

Your phone works out its position from GPS, Bluetooth, crowd-sourced Wi-Fi hotspots and mobile phone towers, Apple says. Android uses a similar mix. The Precise switch doesn't change how your phone finds itself. It changes how much of that answer one app may see.

Android: the numbers Google publishes

Android's developer documentation gives clear figures:

  • Approximate: accurate to within about 3 square kilometres (about 1.2 square miles).
  • Precise: usually within about 50 metres (160 feet), and sometimes within a few metres or better.

If you grant approximate, the app gets approximate, whatever it asked for.

iPhone: what Apple says

Apple's support pages explain the switch but give no size. Its developer documentation does. It says approximate location keeps your country or region, typically keeps your city, and is usually within 1 to 20 kilometres of where you really are. It is blurred in time too: the position updates at most a few times an hour. Apple's 2020 developer talk put it at about four times per hour.

In Apple's 2020 demo, Maps showed the familiar blue dot with Precise on. With Precise off, it showed a large circle instead, with your true position somewhere inside.

You will see other figures online. The numbers here come from Apple's and Google's own documentation, checked on 15 November 2026. Google gives an area and Apple gives a distance range, so they aren't directly comparable.

What stops working when Precise is off

Most apps work fine with a rough area. A few features don't.

  • Turn-by-turn directions. In Apple's own demo, Maps asks for your exact location before it will route you from where you stand.
  • Ride pickups and deliveries. A driver or courier needs your exact pickup point or door, not a rough area.
  • Location-based reminders and geofences. A geofence is an invisible boundary around a place. Your phone tells an app when you cross it, as in "remind me when I leave work." On iPhone, Apple's documentation says an app with reduced accuracy can't use region monitoring, which is Apple's name for geofences. Apple used its own Reminders app as the example. On Android, apps that use Google's geofencing tools must hold the precise location permission.
  • Run and ride tracking. Distance and route maps are built from many exact points. Apple names fitness and navigation apps as the kind of experiences that need full accuracy.
  • Finding a specific spot. If an app only knows your position to within a few kilometres, it can't mark where you parked or which entrance you're at.

You don't have to give up precision forever. On iPhone, an app can ask for a temporary upgrade to full accuracy, which expires automatically some time after you stop actively using the app. On Android, you can grant Only this time, and some apps have a location button that shares your precise location for a single session.

If you build apps, plan for people who keep Precise off, because geofence features won't fire for them. Our guide to testing geofences without driving covers how to check those paths at your desk.

How to change it on iPhone

Precise Location arrived in iOS 14. On any recent iPhone:

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Privacy & Security, then Location Services.
  3. Check that Location Services is on, then scroll down and tap the app.
  4. Choose when the app may use your location (the options are below).
  5. Turn Precise Location off or on.

The access options are:

  • Never: the app gets no location at all.
  • Ask Next Time Or When I Share: the app has to ask again, and you can allow it just once.
  • While Using the App: the app gets your location only while it, or one of its features, is on screen.
  • Always: the app can also read your location in the background.

Some apps offer only two of these. When an app asks for the first time, the permission prompt includes a Precise switch, so you can choose approximate from the start.

How to change it on Android

Per-app control over precise location arrived in Android 12. Menu names vary by phone maker.

Pixel and phones close to standard Android

  1. Open Settings and tap Location.
  2. Tap App location permissions and pick the app.
  3. Choose an access level. Google's help page lists Allow all the time, Allow only while using the app, Ask every time (or allow when you share), and Don't allow.
  4. If you allowed access, turn Use precise location off or on.

A shortcut: touch and hold the app's icon, tap App info, then Permissions > Location.

Samsung Galaxy

  1. Open Settings > Location > App permissions.
  2. Tap the app and choose an access level, such as Allow only while using the app.
  3. On recent One UI versions, a Use precise location switch appears once access is allowed. Turn it off for approximate.

Samsung's own guide covers the first two steps. The precise switch, and a second route through Settings > Apps > the app > Permissions, come from SamMobile's 2026 walkthrough.

When an app first asks

The Android permission prompt lets you pick Precise or Approximate, then While using the app, Only this time, or deny. Only this time is a good fit for an app you rarely use.

Use precise location is not the same as the phone-wide Location Accuracy setting (also called Google Location Services). Location Accuracy lets your phone use Wi-Fi and mobile networks on top of GPS and its sensors. Google warns that turning it off may stop apps getting a precise location at all. To limit one app, use the per-app switch.

Other ways your location gets out

The Precise switch only controls what apps read from your phone's location system. Other routes matter too.

Photos

Photos can carry metadata: hidden details stored inside the file, often called EXIF data. When the camera has location access, that includes where you took the picture, and people you share it with may be able to read it.

  • iPhone, when sharing: select the photos, tap Share, tap Options, and turn off Location. "Location Not Included" then appears above the Options button.
  • iPhone, existing photos: open the photo, tap the More button (three dots), then Adjust Info > Adjust Location > No Location.
  • iPhone, future photos: go to Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services > Camera and choose Never.
  • Android: your camera app can save your location with each photo. To stop that, use the permission steps above to set the camera app to Don't allow. Google also notes that a photo you download and email keeps the location your phone saved.

Even with the data removed, a landmark in the background can give a place away.

Your IP address

Every website and app you connect to sees your IP address, the network address your connection comes from, and it can be matched to a rough area. Google says that even with location turned off, you can still get local results and ads based on your IP address. Our VPN page explains how IP location and GPS location relate.

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth scanning

Android phones can scan for nearby Wi-Fi networks and Bluetooth devices to help apps get better location info. Samsung notes these scans work even when Wi-Fi or Bluetooth is switched off. On Android 12 and later, the switches are at Settings > Location > Location Services > Wi-Fi scanning and Bluetooth scanning. On Samsung phones, look under Settings > Location > Improve accuracy. Turning them off can make location less accurate.

Significant Locations on iPhone

Your iPhone can learn places and routes that matter to you, for suggestions in Maps, Calendar and Photos. To turn this off, go to Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services > System Services > Significant Locations & Routes.

FAQ

Is approximate location the same on iPhone and Android?

No. Android documents approximate location as accurate to within about 3 square kilometres (about 1.2 square miles). Apple says its approximate location typically keeps your city, is usually within 1 to 20 kilometres of you, and updates at most a few times an hour.

Can an app tell that I turned Precise off?

Yes. On iPhone, apps can read whether they have full or reduced accuracy, and they can't opt out of your choice. On Android, apps check which location permission you granted, and Android restarts an app when you downgrade it from precise to approximate.

Does turning off Precise Location stop all tracking?

No. It limits what one app reads from your phone's location system. Websites can still estimate your area from your IP address, photos can carry their own location data, and Apple notes that apps and websites may use other ways to work out where you are.
