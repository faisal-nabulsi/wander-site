Why Sideloaded iPhone Apps Expire After 7 Days (and the 3-App Limit), Explained

Apps you install with a free Apple ID run on a provisioning profile, a permission file from Apple that expires 7 days after it is issued. When it runs out, the app won't open until it is re-signed, and a free account can only have 3 such apps on a device at once.

The short answer

Apple lets anyone with an Apple Account install apps on their own iPhone for free. Apple Account and Apple ID mean the same sign-in here: Apple's pages use the first name, and most sideloading tools, and this post, use the second. In Xcode, Apple's app for building software, this free setup is called a Personal Team. Apple presents it as a way to test your own apps on your own device, and its installs are short-lived: each app's permission to run expires after 7 days, and you can install up to 3 apps per device. Sideloading tools that use a free Apple ID work within those limits, and refreshing is how they keep apps running.

Three terms come up again and again:

  • Sideloading means installing an app from somewhere other than the App Store.
  • A provisioning profile is a file Apple issues to your account. It allows an app signed with your account to be installed on your device.
  • Re-signing means signing the app again with your Apple ID so Apple issues a fresh profile. Refreshing means re-signing and reinstalling the app before the old profile runs out.

Already expired? Do this

Refreshing is meant to happen before day 7. Once the profile has run out, the app won't open, so anything that refreshes from inside that app can't run. You need a computer, or a sideloading tool that still opens.

  1. Don't delete the expired app. SideStore's FAQ, explaining how to move apps over from AltStore or Sideloadly, says to keep the original app on the phone and sideload the same app over it, and its data should stay intact.
  2. If your sideloading tool still opens, refresh or reinstall the app from it. In the bug reports covered below, reinstalling brought apps back.
  3. If the tool itself has expired, reinstall it from a computer first. AltStore and SideStore are sideloaded apps too, and the reports below show them expiring along with the apps they manage. Install AltStore again with AltServer on your computer, or SideStore with iloader, the computer installer its guide uses. Then refresh your other apps from it. With Sideloadly or Xcode, connect the phone and sign the app again from the computer.
  4. For Wander, connect the iPhone to your Mac or PC and run Install Wander again in the Wander Installer, the same way as the first install on the download page.

Apple's rules for free accounts

Apple lists these limits on its Developer account overview page, under its personal team section.

WhatFree Apple Account (Personal Team)
App IDsUp to 10. Each one expires after 7 days.
DevicesUp to 3. Each one expires after 7 days.
Apps per deviceUp to 3.
Provisioning profilesExpire 7 days after they are issued.
After expiryApple says you need to rebuild and reinstall the app.

An App ID is the identifier Apple registers for each app you sign.

The paid Apple Developer Program costs 99 USD a year, according to Apple's enrollment page. Apple's overview page doesn't say how long paid profiles last. Sideloadly and SideStore both say about a year.

Why Apple sets these limits

Apple doesn't give a reason on these pages. It does publish the split between the two tiers. Its enrollment FAQ says you don't need to enroll to install apps on your own device with Xcode. You need to enroll to distribute apps. A profile that lasts a week is fine for testing your own code. It is a poor way to hand out apps, and that is the practical effect of the rule.

The rule isn't new. In September 2016, a reply on Apple's Developer Forums described the one-week expiry for free provisioning as a limit that had arrived that June.

What an expired app looks like

When the profile runs out, the icon stays on your Home Screen, but the app won't open. AltStore's FAQ says that after 7 days, its apps "can no longer be opened." Reports of apps whose signing broke before day 7 (more on those below) describe the same symptoms:

  • The app shows its launch screen, then drops you back to the Home Screen with no error (AltStore issue #806, on iOS 14).
  • iOS shows a message of the form "App name" Is No Longer Available (the same AltStore issue, and SideStore issue #881 from 2025).

We haven't confirmed the wording on the newest iOS release.

Don't confuse it with "Untrusted Developer"

Wander's install guide notes that its first launch shows "Untrusted Developer." That isn't expiry. It means the Apple ID that signed the app hasn't been trusted on the phone yet. Go to Settings → General → VPN & Device Management, tap your Apple ID, and tap Trust.

When an app stops before day 7

Sometimes an app stops opening while the tool still shows days left. Public bug reports point to a few causes:

  • App extensions. Extensions are add-on parts inside an app, such as widgets. They use their own App IDs and have their own expiry. In SideStore issue #887, a developer suspected that extensions which weren't re-signed properly made the whole app invalid.
  • A refresh that didn't really refresh. In the same issue, the tool's day counter reset but the real expiry date didn't change. SideStore marked it fixed in version 0.6.1.
  • The same Apple ID signing on another device. In AltStore issue #806, the person who reported it had a second device signing apps with the same Apple ID. After they reinstalled with a separate Apple ID on each device, the early expiry stopped. If you sign apps on more than one device, giving each device its own Apple ID is worth trying.

In these reports, reinstalling the app brought it back. Refreshing again worked in some cases, but in SideStore issue #887 one user found that refreshing didn't help, and only reinstalling the app file did.

Ways tools handle the 7 days

No tool can make a free profile last longer than 7 days. Tools differ in where the re-signing happens and in what has to be running for it to work.

1. Rebuild with Xcode

This is Apple's intended route, and it is for developers. You open your project in Xcode, connect the phone, and build to it again. It doesn't help with an app you only have as a finished .ipa file, which is the packaged form of an iPhone app.

2. Re-sign from a computer (Sideloadly)

Sideloadly runs on a Mac or a Windows PC. You drop in an .ipa file, enter your Apple ID, and it signs and installs the app. Its FAQ says a background daemon re-signs your apps before they expire. A daemon is a helper program that keeps running on the computer, so the computer has to be on for refreshes.

3. Refresh in the background (AltStore and SideStore)

AltStore puts an alternative app store on the phone. It tries to refresh your apps in the background, and you can tap Refresh All at any time. In AltStore Classic, the refresh goes through AltServer, a companion app on your computer. Its error list tells you to keep the phone on the same Wi-Fi as that computer, or to plug in with USB.

AltStore Classic can now also refresh without your computer. Its Remote AltServers page says you can install and refresh apps from anywhere through a Remote AltServer, a server you pick in AltStore's settings instead of your own computer. Setup includes pairing with a PC once, and after that the phone needs Wi-Fi, not cellular, and LocalDevVPN connected. LocalDevVPN is a free on-device VPN app from the App Store; SideStore, below, uses it too.

SideStore is a community fork of AltStore, meaning a copy of AltStore's code that a separate team develops. It needs a computer only once, during installation. It re-signs apps on the phone itself. For that, it uses LocalDevVPN, which has to be on whenever you install, update or refresh. SideStore's FAQ says this VPN connects only to your own device, with no remote server. To log in to Apple, it uses an anisette server, a server that helps the app sign in to your Apple account. SideStore's FAQ says the most common sign-in error is a temporary anisette server outage.

If a background refresh can't run at any point in the 7 days, the app expires like any other.

4. Pay for the Apple Developer Program

For 99 USD a year, profiles last about a year instead of 7 days, and SideStore says the 3-app cap goes away. You still re-sign, just much less often. It is the right choice if you build apps. For one or two sideloaded apps, it is a big yearly cost.

The 3-app and 10-App-ID limits in practice

3 apps per device

Apple caps a free account at 3 installed apps per device. AltStore describes this as 3 sideloaded apps active at a time. Your sideloading tool can take one of those slots: SideStore notes that the 3 includes SideStore itself. Wander on iPhone is signed with a free Apple ID too, so it uses one of that Apple ID's 3 slots on the phone.

AltStore's answer is to deactivate an app. It backs up the app's data, removes the app, and frees the slot. When you activate it again, AltStore reinstalls the app and restores its data.

SideStore's FAQ also names LiveContainer as a way around the 3-app cap. LiveContainer's README describes it as an app launcher that runs other apps inside itself, using one installed app and one App ID. Its developers warn that unofficial builds of LiveContainer can access the data of every app inside them, so get it from the official project.

10 App IDs per 7 days

Each signed app needs at least one App ID, and its extensions can need more. AltStore's FAQ says the number depends on how many extensions the app contains. You can have 10 registered at a time, and each expires after a week. If you run out, AltStore's advice is to wait until enough expire.

So an app with several extensions can use up your weekly allowance fast. Sideloadly can remove some or all of an app's extensions before installing. That saves App IDs, but any feature that lives in a removed extension won't be there.

The third limit is 3 devices, and those registrations also expire after 7 days. If you sideload onto one iPhone, it rarely comes up.

How Wander handles it

Wander is a location changer for iPhone, Android, Mac and Windows. On iPhone, you install it with your own free Apple ID, so the same 7-day rule applies to it. Like SideStore, Wander refreshes on the phone after a one-time computer setup. There's no jailbreak, no $99 Apple developer account and no card up front; the pricing page has the free plan details. Some apps' terms forbid changing your location, and you're responsible for following them.

One-time setup

The first install needs a Mac or a Windows PC and takes about 10 minutes; the download page has the full guide. The Wander Installer signs Wander with your free Apple ID and installs it on your connected iPhone. On the iPhone, you then trust your Apple ID, turn on Developer Mode (an iPhone setting that controls whether locally installed apps may run), and connect LocalDevVPN on Wi-Fi. The download page covers cellular. If the Developer Mode switch is missing, see Developer Mode not showing on iPhone.

After setup: self-refresh

According to the features page, Wander re-signs and refreshes itself over the air after setup. Over the air means over the internet, without a cable. New versions also download and install themselves. Each refresh still gets an ordinary 7-day profile from Apple, so a refresh has to happen within every 7 days.

In the app, Settings has a Self-refresh section, shown above and in the features page's Pro & self-refresh screenshot. It shows whether Wander is signed in to your Apple ID, and it has a Run self-refresh (sign + install) button. The screen explains that the button signs Wander with your Apple ID and reinstalls it over the tunnel, which resets the 7-day clock without a computer. The tunnel is LocalDevVPN; the download page calls it the secure tunnel Wander uses. A refresh needs much the same things as on SideStore:

  • Your Apple ID signed in inside Wander. Check that the Self-refresh section shows "Apple ID — signed in." If it doesn't, sign in there before you count on a refresh.
  • LocalDevVPN connected, because the refresh reinstalls Wander over it.
  • Apple's sign-in working at that moment. Your Apple ID stays signed in, stored in the iPhone's Keychain, its built-in password storage. Apple may sometimes ask for a two-factor code again.

Wander closes itself when the refresh installs. Just open it again.

The safe habit: open Wander with LocalDevVPN connected and tap Run self-refresh about once a week, and before any stretch when you won't use it. Don't count on a refresh happening while Wander sits unopened. If 7 days pass without a refresh, Wander expires like any other sideloaded app, and you'll need the computer to install it again (see Already expired?).

Within Apple's limits, not around them. Each refresh gets a new 7-day profile from Apple. It doesn't stretch a profile past 7 days or lift the app and App ID limits. It moves the renewal from your desk to your phone.

If Wander won't open

  • If it still opens, run self-refresh now, with LocalDevVPN connected.
  • If it shows its launch screen and closes, or iOS says it's no longer available, it has most likely expired. Follow Already expired? Do this and run the Wander Installer again.

Is signing with your Apple ID safe?

Every tool in this post asks for your Apple ID, because Apple issues the profile to your account. So you are trusting the tool with your account sign-in. A few habits help:

  • Download tools only from their official sites. A modified copy of a signing tool could misuse your sign-in.
  • Prefer open source. Anyone can read open-source code, so someone can check what it does with your credentials. AltStore is licensed under the GNU AGPL. Wander's iPhone app is open source under the GNU AGPL v3, and its desktop app under the GNU GPL v3. The security page links to the source.
  • Know how the tool signs in to Apple. SideStore's FAQ is open about this: the anisette server it signs in through can add a generated Mac to your account's device list, and older, heavily shared anisette servers have been known to cause Apple ID locks. Ask the same question of any tool you use, Wander included.

FAQ

Can you bypass the 7-day limit?

No. With a free Apple ID, every provisioning profile expires 7 days after Apple issues it. Tools can only refresh the app before that happens, which gets a new 7-day profile.

Does the paid $99 developer account remove the limit?

It changes it. The Apple Developer Program costs 99 USD a year. Sideloadly and SideStore say paid profiles last about a year, and SideStore says the 3-app cap no longer applies. You still re-sign, but about once a year instead of every week.

Does an iOS update break sideloaded apps?

An update doesn't change the 7-day rule, which counts from when Apple issued the profile. Each sideloading tool lists the iOS versions it supports, so check that before you update. New versions of Wander download and install themselves over the air, so fixes for iOS changes can reach you without reinstalling from a computer.

Do App Store apps count toward the 3-app limit?

No. The limit covers apps signed with your free Apple ID. AltStore's FAQ describes it as 3 sideloaded apps at a time.
