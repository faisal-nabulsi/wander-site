How to Get Your Real Location Back After a Mock or Simulated Location (iPhone and Android)

To get your real location back, clear the simulated location in the tool that set it, turn location off and on, and restart your phone if needed. Then give GPS several minutes outdoors to find you again; on iPhone, Reset Location & Privacy is the last resort.

Published 15 November 2026 · 11 min read

Quick answer: Clear the simulated location in the tool that set it: in Xcode, choose Don't Simulate Location; on Android, set Select mock location app to Nothing. Then turn location off and on, and restart the phone if the wrong spot remains. Check a maps app outside and give it a few minutes. On iPhone, Reset Location & Privacy is the last resort.

The fix ladder, from gentlest to most disruptive

First, two terms. A simulated location is a made-up position that a developer tool, such as Apple's Xcode, feeds to an iPhone in place of the real one. A mock location is Android's version of the same idea. Google describes the Android setting as a way to fake the device's GPS location so developers can test their apps in other places.

These steps aren't tied to one tool. Work down the list and stop as soon as your real location is back.

  1. Stop or clear it in the tool you used. Look for a Stop, Clear or Don't Simulate option. The iPhone and Android sections list common ones.
  2. Turn location off and on. On iPhone, use Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services. On Android, use Settings > Location > Use location.
  3. Restart the phone. Turn it fully off, then back on.
  4. Check in a maps app, outside. Go somewhere with a clear view of the sky, open a maps app, and wait a few minutes.
  5. iPhone only: Reset Location & Privacy. Keep this for last. It resets your location and privacy settings to their defaults, so apps can't use your location until you allow them again, and your iPhone forgets which computers it trusts.

iPhone: step by step

If you used Xcode

  1. While your app is running from Xcode on the iPhone, choose Debug > Simulate Location > Don't Simulate Location.
  2. Already unplugged? A Stack Overflow answer says to run an app on the phone from Xcode again and choose Don't Simulate Location.
  3. So the next run doesn't start at a fake spot, open Product > Scheme > Edit Scheme. In Options, set Default Location to None.

For other Xcode location problems, see Xcode Simulate Location not working.

If you used pymobiledevice3

pymobiledevice3 is a free command-line tool for working with iPhones from a computer. To end a location you set with it, run its clear command. In a 2023 GitHub issue about iOS 17, the command looked like this. HOST and PORT are the address and port of the tunnel you started, which is a private connection from your computer to the iPhone's developer services:

pymobiledevice3 developer dvt simulate-location clear --rsd HOST PORT

Commands can change between versions, so check the tool's help. Two more tips from the issue:

  • One user found clear failed after they unplugged and reconnected the phone, but setting a new location still worked.
  • Another suggested setting a location close to where you really are, then clearing it. Their reason: iOS can struggle to find you again after a far-away simulated spot.

If you used a desktop app

Check the tool for its own stop control. Two open-source examples:

  • GeoPort (Windows and Mac). If you forgot to reset your location before disconnecting, its GitHub page says to connect the phone again and use Stop Location.
  • iFakeLocation (Windows, Mac and Ubuntu). Click Stop Fake Location. If the phone is still stuck, its GitHub page says to turn Location Services off and on.

If you control your iPhone from a PC, see how to change iPhone location from Windows.

Turn Location Services off and on

  1. Open Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services.
  2. Turn Location Services off and wait a few seconds.
  3. Turn it back on.

Restart the iPhone

Apple suggests turning iPhone off and back on when it isn't working as expected. Go to Settings > General, scroll down, tap Shut Down, and drag the slider. To turn it back on, press and hold the side button until the Apple logo appears.

Last resort: Reset Location & Privacy

  1. Open Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone.
  2. Tap Reset.
  3. Tap Reset Location & Privacy.

Apple says this "resets the location services and privacy settings to their defaults." Afterwards, apps stop using your location until you give them permission again. In Apple's own description, privacy settings also cover which apps can use things like your contacts, photos, camera and microphone. Apple also says this reset makes your iPhone forget which computers it trusts. The next time you connect it to a computer you trusted before, it will ask again whether to trust that computer, so tap Trust before you use tools such as Xcode or pymobiledevice3 with it. That's why this step comes last.

Your content stays. Apple says resetting settings doesn't erase your content. But don't tap Erase All Content and Settings on the Transfer or Reset iPhone screen: Apple warns it removes all your content.

Android: step by step

Menu names vary by phone maker. If you can't find a setting, search for it in the Settings app.

  1. Stop the app. Use the mock location app's own Stop button first.
  2. Set the mock location app to Nothing. Open Developer options and find Select mock location app under Debugging. Choose Nothing. In Android's own Settings code, choosing Nothing takes mock-location permission away from every app. The setting should then read "No mock location app set."
  3. Or switch Developer options off. Use the main switch at the top of the Developer options screen. The same Settings code removes mock-location permission from every app when you do this. It also turns off most other developer settings, such as USB debugging. To use a mock location app again later, you'll need to turn Developer options back on and select the app again.
  4. Force-stop or uninstall the app. Google's steps are: Settings > Apps > See all apps, pick the app, then Force stop > OK. If you don't need the app any more, uninstall it.
  5. Turn Location off and on. Go to Settings > Location and switch Use location off, then on.
  6. Check Location Accuracy. On Android 12 and later, go to Settings > Location > Location Services > Location Accuracy. Make sure Improve Location Accuracy is on. On Android 11 and lower, it's under Settings > Location > Advanced > Location Accuracy. When it's off, Google says only GPS and the phone's sensors are used, which may affect how accurate your location is.
  7. Open a maps app outside and wait. In a 2016 Stack Overflow thread about a mock location that stuck on a Nexus 6P, one answer suggests turning GPS on, opening Google Maps so it asks for a location, and waiting, not indoors.
  8. Restart the phone.

Can't find the setting? See 'Select mock location app' not showing on Android.

Why a simulated location can stick

If you want to know why the steps above work, a simulated location usually sticks for one of three reasons. Some of the reports below are several years old, so we've noted the year or software version where the source makes it clear.

It was never cleared

A simulated location lasts until something clears it, and unplugging the phone doesn't always count. In a 2014 Stack Overflow question, a developer testing on an iPhone 4 unplugged it after using Xcode and still saw the simulated spot. Toggling location and reinstalling the app didn't help. The accepted answer included turning the phone off and back on. On Apple's developer forum, one developer says a location set in Xcode was gone after a reboot. It may depend on the iOS version: another developer says that on iOS 18 the simulation was lost when the iPhone was disconnected, though it used to persist.

Clearing didn't work

In a 2016 Apple Developer Forums thread about Xcode 8, a developer turned off location simulation, but the phone still reported London, the simulated spot. Rebooting the phone fixed it. A later reply reports the same problem, with the fake spot showing up in other apps too. In the 2023 pymobiledevice3 issue about iOS 17, the tool's clear command sometimes did nothing: the phone stayed at the simulated spot even though the logs said simulation had stopped. The maintainer couldn't reproduce it and said it might be a bug on Apple's side. The issue is now closed.

Your real location is coming back slowly

Even when clearing works, the phone has to find you again. It works out where you are from GPS satellites, Wi-Fi networks, cell towers and Bluetooth. Apple says finding all visible GPS satellites can take several minutes, and accuracy improves gradually. In the same pymobiledevice3 issue, the user sometimes saw the real location return as a circle kilometres wide that took many minutes to sharpen. And in 2016, a developer testing on a Nexus 6P with Android 6.0.1 found that after turning off a mock location app, Google Play services' Fused Location Provider kept returning the old location. The Fused Location Provider is a Google location tool that combines signals such as GPS and Wi-Fi to work out where the phone is.

How to tell your real location is back

Open a maps app and look at your dot. Apple explains that when Maps can't pin you down precisely, it shows a blue circle around the marker. The smaller the circle, the more precise the location. If the circle is large, give it time.

  • Get a clear view of the sky. Apple says walls, roofs and tall buildings can block GPS signals. When that happens, the phone uses Wi-Fi or cell networks until it can see the satellites again.
  • Set the date and time automatically. Apple lists a correct date, time and time zone as a way to improve GPS accuracy. On iPhone, use Settings > General > Date & Time and turn on Set Automatically.
  • Give it several minutes. Apple says accuracy improves gradually.

For what the different accuracy levels mean, see precise vs approximate location.

Still wrong? It may not be the tool

Wi-Fi and cell data. Indoors, phones lean on nearby Wi-Fi networks and cell towers. Apple and Google both use crowd-sourced data about where those networks are. Apple also warns that location data services can be inaccurate or incomplete in some areas. Check your location again outside, where GPS can reach you.

Your IP address. Your IP address is the network address your connection comes from, and services can use it to guess roughly where you are. Google notes that even with Location off, you can still get local results and ads based on your IP address. If you set up a VPN to match a place you were testing, disconnect it. Our guide to GPS and IP location explains how the two differ.

FAQ

Will restarting always fix a stuck location?

Not always, but it's worth trying early. In Apple's developer forum, a restart fixed a location that Xcode had left stuck. Clear the location in the tool you used first, then restart. If that doesn't help on iPhone, Reset Location & Privacy is the last resort.

Does Reset Location & Privacy delete my data?

No. Apple says you can return iPhone settings to their defaults without erasing your content. Reset Location & Privacy resets location services and privacy settings, so apps stop using your location until you give them permission again. It does make your iPhone forget which computers it trusts, so you'll need to tap Trust again when you next connect it to one. Don't confuse it with Erase All Content and Settings, which removes all your content.

How long until location accuracy is normal again?

It can take several minutes. Apple says finding all visible GPS satellites can take several minutes, with accuracy improving over time. Go outside with a clear view of the sky, and set the date, time and time zone automatically to help.
