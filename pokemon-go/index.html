The difference that matters

We never modify
the game.

PGSharp, iPogo and the old Global++ apps all work the same way: they hand you a rebuilt copy of Pokémon GO. That modified client is what Niantic detects, and it's what Niantic sues over. Wander doesn't touch the game at all.

What Wander does

  • Changes the location your phone reports, at the system level — over Apple's own developer tunnel on iPhone, and the built-in mock-location developer setting on Android.
  • Leaves you on the real Pokémon GO from the App Store or Play Store — Niantic's build, Niantic's signature, updating on its own.
  • Everything on the phone reads that same system location: maps, weather, the game. Nothing about it is game-specific.

What Wander never does

  • Never patches, repacks or re-signs Pokémon GO, and never asks you to install a version of it that didn't come from the store.
  • Never injects code into the running game or hooks its internals.
  • Never touches your trainer account or Niantic's servers — no sign-in, no bot, no auto-catching, no IV or spawn data pulled out of the game.

Straight with you: this is not a promise that you can't be banned. Changing your location can break Niantic's terms all on its own, and moving in ways a human couldn't is detectable behaviour no matter how the location got there — which is exactly why Wander ships a cooldown timer, GPS jitter and real walking speeds. What we can say precisely is what Wander doesn't do. Niantic sued Global++ over its modified Pokémon GO, Ingress and Wizards Unite clients; in January 2021 Global++ agreed to a $5 million settlement and a permanent injunction. Error 12

"Failed to detect
location (12)"

The message that stops most iPhone spoofers dead. Here's why it happens, what Wander does about it, and — just as important — where that answer runs out.

The cause

It's a flag, not your setup

The usual way to move an iPhone's location is Apple's developer tunnel. It works fine — but iOS marks every fix that comes out of it as software-simulated, and recent Pokémon GO builds reject fixes carrying that mark. Nothing you can reinstall or re-pair changes it, which is why the same error follows people from tool to tool.

Wander's answer

Anti-cheat games mode

Wander has a second, separate engine on iPhone. Rather than injecting a location, it changes what Apple's Wi-Fi positioning service reports about the networks around you, and lets iOS work out the fix by itself the ordinary way. A fix iOS calculated isn't marked as simulated — so the game accepts it. It's a whole-app switch, not a per-game one: while anti-cheat mode is on, every teleport in Wander goes through this engine, and you run the proxy app's VPN instead of LocalDevVPN (iOS allows only one at a time). Turn it back off for everything else.

The proof

One tap: "Check my spoof"

Wander asks iOS where Wander is — through the exact pipeline the game reads — and compares it to the spot you asked for. Green means it's landing end to end; amber tells you it hasn't taken yet and what to do about it. No squinting at Apple Maps trying to guess.

Where it stops — please read this before you set it up

  • It steers network positioning, not GPS. A strong real GPS fix overrides it. This mode holds indoors or anywhere GPS is weak — it is not a way to walk around outside under open sky.
  • It's for sitting still and hopping between spots, and you switch it off again afterwards. Because it's a whole-app switch, leaving it on sends Find My, Life360, dating apps and multi-stop routes through the same indoor, teleport-only engine — turn it off for those and Wander's regular engine handles them properly, with no proxy and no certificate.
  • Setup is real work, and it isn't free. You install a supported proxy app from the App Store (Shadowrocket, Loon, Quantumult X or Stash — all paid; Shadowrocket is the one we've tested end to end), import Wander's config, then install and trust a certificate. Wander walks you through every step, and shows you exactly how to turn it all off again — one tap from the right Settings screen.
  • Take the certificate seriously. While it's trusted it can decrypt HTTPS across your whole device, not just for the game. Wander states that plainly during setup and makes you acknowledge it before it hands you the button. If that isn't a trade you want to make, leave this mode off — everything else in Wander works without it.
  • Each new spot needs a flush. iOS only picks up the new position after Location Services is toggled off for a full ten seconds or so and back on. Wander puts you one tap from that screen, but no app is allowed to flip that switch for you.
  • It's marked experimental in the app, off by default, and Apple can change the behaviour underneath it with any iOS release. We'd rather you knew that now than after you'd paid.
